About WDI

Residential capacity for AI data collection.

What WDI does

Web Data Infrastructure buys residential capacity from cloud platforms and network owners, then deploys and operates nodes supplying the Grass network. We work with providers that already have pools of residential connections, so supply can be purchased as an agreed allocation.

The provider supplies the connections and underlying compute. WDI handles procurement, client deployment, monitoring and recovery. Public-web data collected through the network supports downstream AI training and retrieval products.

Infrastructure and data networks

Our deployment and evaluation work spans SaladCloud, Akash, Flux Cloud and NexQloud. Each allocation is assessed for its network origin, countries, runtime environment and permitted workload. Grass is the primary data network served by our node operations; Wynd Labs develops data products and provides core engineering and software services within that ecosystem.

Deployment and support

The supported node client sends and receives public-web requests through the allocated connections. The provider supplies the network access; the data network manages collection and downstream processing. WDI runs the deployment between them. Grass explains this request flow in its connection guide.

Before deployment
WDI and the provider agree the client, locations, allocation size, resource and bandwidth limits, access method and supply period. The commercial agreement also identifies the billing basis and operational contacts.
Provider responsibilities
The provider controls the underlying machines and connections. It supplies the agreed resources, confirms that the workload is allowed and explains how outages, maintenance and replacement allocations are handled.
WDI responsibilities
WDI installs and configures the agreed client, maintains its runtime configuration and follows node health and traffic. Recovery work preserves the allocation and node identity where possible; changes to the underlying supply are coordinated with the provider.
Monitoring
Operating records track received and sent traffic, source timestamps and deployment history. These readings support performance review and investigation when an allocation stops reporting or behaves differently.
Changes and incidents
The deployment agreement defines how to report an issue, who can pause the workload and how replacement capacity is approved. A provider can raise a concern directly through contact@webdatainfrastructure.com, with the affected service and time.

Grass sets the rules for use of its network in its Network Use Policy. The provider’s own restrictions and the agreed WDI deployment scope also apply to the allocation.

Traffic and coverage data

The homepage figures describe WDI’s own measured operations. They update from the same operating records used by the traffic graph.

30-day and 24-hour traffic
Each figure adds received and sent bytes within that rolling time window. As time moves forward, new measurements enter the window and older ones leave it. The totals include network and protocol traffic across the workloads measured by WDI.
Unique IPs
Each different public address observed across WDI operations is counted once. Repeated observations and the same address appearing in multiple recording systems do not create additional IPs. A supply quotation states the allocation available for the proposed deployment.
Countries and regions
Coverage is counted from the country information attached to observed public addresses. Each location appears once in the total. The countries required for a new allocation are specified with the provider as part of the supply agreement.
Reading the graph
The 30-day view shows received and sent traffic in hourly buckets; the 24-hour view uses minute buckets. The five-minute and one-hour views show current traffic rates. Select a point to inspect its time, received volume and sent volume.
Units and updates
Units are decimal: 1 GB is 1,000 MB and 1 TB is 1,000 GB. Traffic history refreshes each minute; current rates refresh from the active feeds. The date beneath the graph identifies the latest observation. A missing observation is left as a gap.

Business and operator

WDI is a privately funded infrastructure business based in India, operated by CosmosGeek. Procurement and operating enquiries go directly to contact@webdatainfrastructure.com.

The contracting name, billing details, capacity, pricing and responsibilities are confirmed before a paid engagement.

AI data demand

AI pre-training and multimodal model development use public-web text, images, audio and video. Live Context Retrieval supplies fresh web information for search and inference. Grass describes these directions in its network update.

Video annotation also supports robotics and perception. Grass and Inference.net’s published video-annotation work describes applications including autonomous vehicles and warehouse robotics. WDI supplies and operates residential access used by the collection network.
